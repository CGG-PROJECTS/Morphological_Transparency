Morphological Transparency Norms Dashboard

Project Summary

This dataset provides large-scale estimates of semantic transparency and perceived morphological structure for English polymorphemic words.

  • Study 1: Semantic distance between a derivation and its baseword (Nagy & Anderson, 1984).
  • Study 2: Crowdsourced human norms and fine-tuned LLM estimates of perceived morphemes.

Understanding the Metrics

Study 1: Semantic Distance Scale

Ratings 0–2 are considered transparent (meaning can be inferred), while 3–5 are considered opaque.

  • SEM 0: Semantically transparent.
  • SEM 1: Meaning inferable from baseword with minimal help from context.
  • SEM 2: Meaning inferable with reasonable help from context.
  • SEM 3: Meaning includes features not inferable without substantial context.
  • SEM 4: Distantly related; probably not apparent unless pointed out.
  • SEM 5: Lack of any discernible semantic connection.

Study 2: Perceived Morphemes & Discrepancy

  • Perceived Morpheme Count: The average number of meaningful word parts readers typically perceive in a word.
  • Signed Discrepancy (Perceived minus ELP): Negative values indicate undersegmentation (readers perceived fewer morphemes than listed). Positive values indicate oversegmentation (readers perceived more morphemes).
  • Absolute Discrepancy: The total size of the mismatch. Only a value of 0 indicates perfect alignment between human perception and lexical databases (transparent). Any value ≥ 1 indicates mismatch (opaque).
  • Transparency Proximity: A 0-1 scaled score where 1 equals perfect alignment.
